      DieRoute: Multi-Die Routing with Partitioned Authority
                                    Evidence and Research Draft

                         Research draft; authorship and venue pending
                                            14 October 2026

                                                  Abstract
         DieRoute studies cross-die routing using SLL reservation, die-local resource authority, versioned
     boundary summaries, and HBM placement. The prototype reuses PersistRoute search and transac-
     tional commit infrastructure. We report verified component behavior, native-graph snapshot searches,
     and actual compute unit execution in hardware emulation. Separate resource buffers map three owner
     shards to HBM groups 13, 14, and 15. These results establish bounded functional properties of a
     serialized prototype. They do not establish an asynchronous multi-owner architecture or board per-
     formance. Development workloads remain above the frozen native wirelength baseline. This draft
     therefore records unresolved research gates rather than a completed accelerator evaluation.


1    Problem and scope
Cross-die connections couple local resource occupancy to scarce boundary resources. The research objec-
tive is to route complete designs while preserving native legality, timing and wirelength, and to obtain
an independently measured end-to-end accelerator benefit. PersistRoute provides the reused search, ver-
sioned validation and commit substrate; reuse is not claimed as DieRoute novelty. The proposed research
contributions are SLL reservation and corridor repair, single-owner resource state with boundary sum-
maries, HBM/die mapping, and criticality/congestion-aware migration. The latter contributions require
additional integration and ablation evidence.


2    Authority and search protocol
For an electrical routing resource r, exactly one logical owner stores its authoritative occupancy, history,
version and membership. A boundary summary is read-only information derived from that authority;
it must not become a second independent writer. Physical die membership and authority ownership
are separate concepts, particularly for resources spanning multiple dies. The prototype uses a canonical
owner within the membership set.
    A proposal records each consumed resource observation and its version. Publication requires com-
patible session/epoch identity, valid graph edges and immutable admission policy, and current consumed
versions. A failed proposal must preserve all authority bytes. Multi-sink branches attach to the existing
net tree, and shared resources count once per net. These requirements apply also to rejected search,
malformed paths and late commit overflow.
    The present banked implementation has three separate resource AXI interfaces and shared tree, net
and control state. Searches consume immutable authority snapshots; commit follows under exclusive
serialized handoff. The search and commit compute units share the same device buffers. This implemen-
tation is a functional step toward the architecture: independent buffers do not prove concurrent writers,
distributed ordering or asynchronous shadow coherence.


3    Corridor selection and development variants
The routing reference ranks candidate portals using a timing-weighted tree seed cost, a source-side ge-
ometric estimate, transition cost and destination lookahead. Detailed searches subsequently realize the
corridor and stage the complete branch before publication. Failed reservations are released before re-
pair or fallback. Reservation does not by itself demonstrate fewer global SLL crossings or a hardware
performance benefit.

   The development entry-cost variant replaces the source geometric estimate with a local multi-source
search on the same resource snapshot. It uses the reference single-label cost/hop ordering; it is not a
Pareto solver for hop-constrained paths. Both upstream and downstream work are included in total
expansions and edge visits. A second variant stops upstream traversal once all eligible portal sources
have finalized labels. Nonnegative edge costs justify retaining these distances under the same single-label
ordering. Component tests compare selected paths and complete authority with full traversal; native
repeated evaluation of this stopping rule remains in progress.


4    Evidence levels and methodology
We distinguish CPU reference execution, synthesizable top C-model execution, RTL cosimulation, XRT
compute unit execution in hardware emulation, routed implementation and physical board measurement.
Evidence at one level is not substituted for another. Hardware emulation wall time is not a board speed
or energy metric. Initial native authority imports are reported explicitly.
    The VTR architecture-model development cases are diffeq, alu4 and ex5p. Algorithm choices may use
these cases. Reserved validation cases remain unopened until an algorithm/configuration freeze. Current
commercial evidence uses native U280 topology and selected rerouting cohorts; it does not constitute a
representative held-out full-design cohort. Repeated runs check deterministic artifacts and counters in
addition to native validation.


5    Verified functional results
A banked top C-model search on a native cross-die snapshot expands 229746 nodes and consumes 862428
observations, with path cost 42. Exported search artifacts match the independently audited flat reference.
This case has no occupied observations and does not prove live contention or asynchronous routing.
    A serialized small hardware-emulation fixture launches 16 actual compute unit calls. Runtime owner
groups are 13, 14 and 15. Stale validation is rejected, a valid path is published, and authority is not
reuploaded. A separate native smoke case uploads and verifies 3746844664 bytes of graph/authority data,
performs seed/search/commit/query calls and independently checks all 341 net trees after publication.
It expands only 2 nodes; large input capacity and a short search must not be conflated with a substantial
native routing execution.

    The geometry-weight ablation compares weights 0 and 64 on ex5p. Five audited repeats give byte-
identical results for the seven common canonical artifacts. Native wirelength and CPD are unchanged.
This result does not support adopting a different geometry weight for this development case.
    Fresh native delay extraction covers 5980 final connections across the three cases. Local-RC path
sums differ from full routed-tree delay by at most 1–2 ps. These observations concern final paths, not all
intermediate iterations or arbitrary architectures, and are not a causal explanation of QoR.
    The entry-cost variant passes five independently audited deterministic repeats. All routes are native-
legal; wirelength is 10749 and CPD is 9.67608 ns. It reduces convergence from 22 to 13 iterations but
expands 96811142 nodes, about 2.85 times the original downstream work. Wirelength remains about
11.23% above baseline and CPD about 0.79% above baseline. These repeated results neither close the
QoR gate nor support a speedup claim.

G1–G3 cover frozen inputs, complete routing and representative workload coverage. G4 requires joint
native legality, SLL, timing and wirelength acceptance. G5 requires reservation, mapping, shadow, mi-
gration and topology ablations. G6 requires the integrated hardware validation and routed timing and
resource evidence. G7 requires physical board end-to-end performance, energy and repeated measure-
ments. G8 requires the paper, full claim matrix, reproducible artifact bundle and requirement-wide final
audit. This draft and the direct evidence inventory are partial G8 artifacts.
    Physical U280 execution is unavailable in the current environment. Routed implementation, large
native hardware-emulation searches and repeated development variants are still being evaluated. A
complete literature review, novelty positioning, final architectural figures, held-out results, full artifact
packaging and final claim review are required before submission.
